LAW 1 · THE WALL

The flat line is the wall. The sloping line is the pressure. Pressure rising into a ceiling breaks up. Pressure falling onto a floor breaks down.

LAW 2 · THE VERDICT

No close, no trade. Wicks are rumors. Mid-bar prices are rumors. The close is the only verdict.

01 / Why walls breakLAW 1

The wall is a real seller

A triangle is not geometry. It's an auction with a seller parked at one price — a resting order with real inventory. Every touch of the flat line eats some of it. Watch the wall's size on the ladder. When the inventory hits zero, there is nothing left to absorb the buying — and the break is not a prediction, it's a vacuum.

DEPTH THE AUCTION
READY Press play. The amber row is a seller holding 480 contracts at 100.0.

What this buys you forever

  • Why ascending breaks up: the seller at the ceiling runs out of contracts while buyers keep arriving from higher and higher prices.
  • Why the throwback happens (~64%): after the wall breaks, the level flips — trapped shorts cover there and new buyers defend it. The old ceiling becomes the floor. That's why your stop never lives at the line.
  • Why the pennant fails: run it and look at the ladder. No resting size anywhere. Nothing to eat. Nothing to break. The "trigger" moved every bar because there was never a defended price. Bulkowski ranks it 36th of 39 — this is the reason.

03 / The long patternLAW 1LAW 2

Ascending Triangle

Now watch the auction from Section 01 drawn as candles. The tape will stop on the decision bar and ask for your call before it shows you the answer — because the only rep that counts is the one made at the blank right edge.

63%break upward
~17%break-even failure
~70%reach target
64%throw back first

Rules

  1. Draw only the flat line. The rising floor confirms; it never triggers.
  2. Take the break 50–75% of the way to the apex. A tip break is exhausted.
  3. Entry: a 2-minute candle closes above the wall.
  4. Stop below the last rising low — never at the wall. Two in three come back to kiss it.
  5. Target: triangle height added to the wall.

04 / The short patternLAW 1LAW 2

Descending Triangle

The mirror. A buyer holds the floor and sellers grind him down. Same auction, upside down. Same question at the decision bar.

64%break downward
57%pull back first

Rules

  1. Draw only the flat floor. Two or more lows at one price.
  2. Entry: a 2-minute candle closes below it.
  3. Stop above the last lower high. The pullback to the old floor is expected.
  4. Context doubles it: descending inside a downtrend beats descending inside an uptrend.
  5. Best version: the flat line is also the opening-range low or the 10:30 initial-balance low. A wall everyone marked before the open is a bigger wall.

05 / The centerpieceLAW 2

The Liar Bar

This tape contains one bar that will lie to you while it is still forming. Mid-bar, price will be above the wall — and the app will stop right there, mid-lie, and ask what you do. Then it shows you both futures: the trader who believed the rumor, and the one who waited for the verdict.

Same chart. Same pattern. One rule apart.

The wick buyer: entered 100.6 mid-bar on the rumor. The bar closed back inside, the wall held, and the stop hit at 99.5. −11 ticks.

The close-waiter: did nothing when the wick lied, entered on the real close at 100.8 six bars later, and rode the trail. +14 ticks and running.

That's a 25-tick spread produced by one sentence: no close, no trade. Every fake-out you've ever been caught in was this bar wearing a different date.

06 / No line to drawLAW 3

The 20 EMA Bounce

Here the wall comes to price. The 20 EMA is a moving shelf of the last 40 minutes of consensus — and in a healthy trend, this is often the very same trade as your flag, because the flag's lower boundary sits right on it. Not a fifth pattern. A second way of seeing the one you already trade.

Rules

  1. The EMA must slope your way. Flat = chop in a costume = stand down.
  2. The low must touch or pierce the EMA. No touch, no test.
  3. The close must be back on the right side of it.
  4. The close must clear the prior candle's high. Next section shows why this one carries the weight.
  5. Stop below the pullback swing low — the EMA moves; your stop shouldn't.

07 / Your question, answered properlyLAW 2LAW 3

How far above the EMA?

Wrong question — and now you know why: distance to a moving line is a rumor too. The line moves again next bar. Measure the candle instead. Drag the close and watch which conditions survive.

CLOSE +0.0
✓

✓

Close is above the EMA The test was passed

✓

Close clears the prior candle's high Buyers took control — didn't just hold on

✓

STAND DOWN

Why condition 3 carries the weight

It scales itself. A close above the prior bar's high means the same thing on a dead M2K tape and a fast one, in July and October — no tick number to memorize or re-tune. If you insist on a number, 0.25 × ATR(14) on the 2-minute self-calibrates — but that's my judgment call, not a tested figure.

Honesty note

No published research measures close-distance-above-EMA on intraday futures. I looked, twice. The four-condition test is reasoned, not backtested. A disciplined default until your journal says otherwise.

08 / Why 200 EMA bounces are rareLAW 3

How much time is in that line?

Arithmetic, not statistics — and once you see it to scale you can't unsee it. The grey bar is one entire trading day.

One RTH session390 min · 9:30–4:00 ET
9 EMA18 min
20 EMA40 min
200 EMA6.7 hrs

The punchline

On the 2-minute, the 200 EMA holds more time than the whole trading day — it drags overnight price into your entry chart. On the 5-minute it spans multiple sessions.

In a real trend, price returns to the 20 over and over — that is the trend. Price reaches the 200 maybe zero to two times a day, and when it does, something has broken. You cannot have both setups at once: if price traveled all the way back to the 200, the trend that powered the 20 EMA bounce is already gone. The 200 is a gate, not a trampoline.

09 / A new job for the 9LAW 3

The Tightness Grader

As an entry gate the 9 EMA is nearly redundant — eighteen minutes of price hugging the candles, agreeing with gates you already have. Gates that agree don't filter. But tightness is worth ~30 percentage points while any MA filter is worth 5–10, and the 9 gives you the first objective definition of tight you've ever had. Drag the pullback deeper.

FLAG TIGHTNESS GRADE A
DEPTH shallow

Grade A · full size

The pullback never closes below the 9. Buyers aren't giving back even eighteen minutes of ground. This is the tight flag the statistics were built on.

9 grades · 20 confirms · 200 gates

  • Never closes below the 9 → GRADE A → full size
  • Closes below the 9, holds the 20 → GRADE B → half size
  • Closes below the 20 → NO TRADE — it isn't a flag anymore
  • Drifts sideways past 15 bars (30 min) → it has become a rectangle. Box rules: close beyond the edge, target the height.

One caution: the 9 grades the setup. It never manages the trade — your structural trail (4–6 ticks beyond swing) stays exactly as it is.

The Nine Lines

  1. The flat line is the wall; the sloping line is the pressure. Ascending = LONG. Descending = SHORT. Unsure? Read the shape, not the name.
  2. Only the wall triggers. Draw the flat line; let the slope confirm.
  3. Break at 50–75% toward the apex. Tip breaks are exhausted.
  4. No close, no trade. Wicks are rumors, mid-bar prices are rumors — the close is the verdict.
  5. Stops live at the last swing, never at the wall. Two in three come back to kiss it, because the broken wall flips into the other side's floor.
  6. The slope is the edge. Flat EMA = chop in a costume = stand down.
  7. 9 grades · 20 confirms · 200 gates. Never bounce the 200.
  8. Fifteen sideways bars and it's a rectangle now. Box rules: close beyond the edge, target the height.
